# Data Card: Compliance Copilot Sprint Datasets

This Data Card is the deliverable named in `Challenge-Project-Overview.md`'s Dataset section: "A documentation artifact explaining dataset curation, gap distribution, and labeling criteria." It covers the datasets this team curated during the **Access Data** (issue #1) and **Data Triage** (issue #3) sprint. It does **not** cover the seeded ground-truth benchmark that the Challenge Advisor will separately provide under the **Benchmark** milestone (issue #4) — see the "Gap Distribution" section below for why those two things must not be confused.

---

## 1. Dataset Composition

### 1.1 NASA guidance corpus

| File | Rows | Source | Content |
|---|---|---|---|
| `data/npr7150_clauses.csv` | 34 | NASA NPR 7150.2D, Chapter 4 "Software Engineering Life Cycle Requirements" (live NODIS HTML page) | Structured requirement clauses (`clause_id`, `npr_section`, `chapter`, `requirement_text`, `applicability`, `source_url`) |
| `data/swehb_5_09_guidance.csv` | 24 | NASA-HDBK-2203, SWEHB Topic 5.09 "SRS - Software Requirements Specification" (live Confluence page) | Structured guidance concepts (`topic`, `guidance_text`, `category`, `source_url`) |

**Extraction method:** Both files are generated by `src/corpus/extract_npr_clauses.py`. Per that script's documented decision (its module docstring), the team deliberately parses NASA's clean, semantic-HTML mirrors of both documents (via `requests` + `BeautifulSoup`/`lxml`) rather than PDF-mining the source PDFs, because: (1) no PDF-parsing tooling was available and the team chose not to add a new dependency to work around that; (2) tag-structure-aware HTML parsing is far more reliable than PDF text-mining, which is prone to broken ligatures and lost heading structure; (3) NPR Chapter 4 paragraphs follow one very regular `"<section#> <requirement sentence> [SWE-xxx]"` pattern, letting a single anchored regex do the extraction; (4) the script is re-runnable and auditable — same URL in, same structured CSV out, no manual copy/paste; (5) it falls back to embedded reference data if the live NASA sites are unreachable, so the pipeline never breaks on a network failure. The extraction is scoped to **Chapter 4 of NPR 7150.2D only** (see Limitations, §5).

**Content-fidelity QA:** `data/npr7150_clauses_QA.md` documents an independent, non-trusting re-verification pass: all 34 NPR rows and all 24 SWEHB rows (not a sample) were diffed against freshly re-fetched live source pages (verbatim text, section numbers, and applicability), plus an independent structural re-check (row counts, null counts, duplicate counts). Result: **34/34 and 24/24 rows confirmed accurate, zero factual errors found.** One minor completeness note was flagged but not corrected: the `swehb_5_09_guidance.csv` row `"Decomposed vs Derived Requirements"` only defines *Decomposed* requirements and omits *Derived* requirements, even though its topic label implies both — the content present is accurate, just incomplete relative to its own title. **Overall verdict: ready for downstream use.**

### 1.2 Starhawk Mission Computer SRS

`data/Starhawk Mission Computer SRS.md` — a **54-requirement** synthetic Software Requirements Specification (`SFMC-REQ-001`–`SFMC-REQ-054`, Doc ID SH-SRS-001), covering a fictional spacecraft mission computer's Navigation, Propulsion, Targeting/Weapons, Defensive, Communications, and Vehicle Health subsystems, plus Emergency Operation, Performance, Reliability, Data, Security, and Constraints sections.

**Why fictional:** Per the Sprint Packet's own framing, this project does not require aerospace domain expertise — the task is evaluating *requirement-writing quality* (is a requirement specific, testable, measurable, unambiguous) against NASA's documentation standards, not evaluating spacecraft engineering feasibility. A fictional SFMC lets the team practice and validate the review methodology against realistic-shaped requirements (numeric thresholds, mode-conditioned behavior, safety interlocks, vague/undefined language seeded intentionally by whoever authored the SRS) without needing real, potentially sensitive mission data or subject-matter expertise the team doesn't have.

## 2. Labeling Criteria

### 2.1 ACCEPTABLE / NEEDS REVIEW

Each of the 54 Starhawk requirements was manually reviewed once and labeled in `docs/manual_requirement_review.csv`:

| Assessment | Count |
|---|---|
| ACCEPTABLE | 35 |
| NEEDS REVIEW | 19 |

A requirement was labeled **ACCEPTABLE** when its meaning, scope, and verification method were all clear enough that a tester could write a concrete pass/fail test directly from the sentence. It was labeled **NEEDS REVIEW** when it depended on an undefined term, an unquantified performance/capacity attribute, an unverifiable absolute/escape clause, or bundled independently-verifiable obligations into one sentence — i.e., when no objective pass/fail test could be written from the text as given. Every NEEDS REVIEW row also records an Issue Category, an Explanation, a NASA Source citation (an SWEHB 5.09 clause, never an NPR §4.1 clause — see §3 below), and, where possible, a Proposed Revision.

### 2.2 Five-category taxonomy

The 19 NEEDS REVIEW rows were generalized into exactly five recurring problem types, defined with detection questions in `docs/requirement_quality_taxonomy.md`:

| # | Category | Count |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Undefined Term | 11 |
| 2 | Unquantified Performance Criterion | 4 |
| 3 | Unverifiable Behavior | 2 |
| 4 | Compound Requirement | 1 |
| 5 | Missing Numerical Bound | 1 |
| | **Total** | **19** |

No category was invented beyond what the 19 rows actually showed, and none of the five present in the data was omitted from the taxonomy.

## 3. Gap Distribution — Important Distinction

`Challenge-Project-Overview.md`'s Dataset section describes a future **ground-truth benchmark**: a "Student-created software requirements set, intentionally containing compliant items and seeded non-compliance gaps," to be provided by the **Challenge Advisor along with its own accompanying Data Card**, under the **Benchmark** milestone (GitHub issue #4).

**The 35/19 ACCEPTABLE/NEEDS REVIEW distribution and the 5-category breakdown documented in this Data Card are NOT that ground-truth benchmark.** They are this team's own **independent manual review** of the Starhawk SRS, performed as part of Data Triage (issue #3), before the Challenge-Advisor-provided benchmark exists. This review has two purposes: (1) it develops and validates the review methodology (the taxonomy, the labeling criteria, the citation discipline) that a future automated system will need to reproduce; (2) it may later be compared against the official ground-truth benchmark once issue #4 delivers it, as a cross-check — but it is not a substitute for it and should not be cited as "the" seeded-gap benchmark. Anyone consuming this repository's data should treat `docs/manual_requirement_review.csv` as a team-produced reference review, and wait for the separate, Challenge-Advisor-provided benchmark (with its own Data Card) for the project's official ground truth.

## 4. Related Artifacts

- `docs/requirement_rewrites.md` — 6 fully worked rewrites of NEEDS REVIEW requirements, spanning all 5 taxonomy categories, following a no-fabrication rule (missing facts are left as `[... TBD by stakeholder]` placeholders).
- `docs/nasa_guidance_analysis.csv` — classifies every corpus row by whether it is checkable at the INDIVIDUAL (single-requirement), SET (whole-document), PROCESS (PM activity), or MULTIPLE level; this classification is what grounds every NASA Source citation in the manual review.
- `data/srs_clause_triage.csv` — per-requirement (all 54 rows) listing of which corpus clauses are applicable guidance for that requirement, with filtering rationale.
- `docs/ai_review_brainstorm.md` and `docs/architecture/future_agent_design.md` — brainstorm and early design work for how a future automated reviewer would consume these datasets; no runtime agent code exists yet.

## 5. Known Limitations

- **Single synthetic SRS.** All 54 labeled requirements come from one fictional document (Starhawk Mission Computer SRS). No results here should be assumed to generalize to a different SRS, a different domain, or real (non-synthetic) NASA mission software without further validation.
- **One reviewer pass, not cross-reviewed.** The 54-row manual review and the resulting taxonomy reflect a single reviewer's pass through the SRS. The Sprint Packet's own recommended workflow calls for multiple team members to independently review and reconcile findings; that cross-review has not yet happened for this dataset, so inter-rater agreement is unknown and some ACCEPTABLE/NEEDS REVIEW calls may be contestable.
- **NPR corpus scoped to Chapter 4 only.** `data/npr7150_clauses.csv` covers NPR 7150.2D Chapter 4 ("Software Engineering Life Cycle Requirements") exclusively — it is not a corpus of the full NPR 7150.2D document, which contains additional chapters not extracted this sprint.
- **Not the official ground-truth benchmark.** As stated in §3, the ACCEPTABLE/NEEDS REVIEW distribution here is this team's own review, not the seeded ground-truth benchmark the Challenge Advisor will provide under issue #4. Downstream consumers must not conflate the two.
